# Format and length

Use a single column with a clear top-to-bottom reading order. Standard headings and plain text contact details work for recruiters, screen readers, and applicant tracking systems.

One page is often enough for an early-career candidate. Two pages are reasonable when the second page contains relevant evidence. Do not shrink body text below 10 points to force a page count. Cut repetition first.

Use:

- one readable typeface;
- consistent dates and punctuation;
- ordinary bullets;
- enough white space to separate sections;
- PDF for submission unless the employer asks for another format.

Avoid:

- photos unless local hiring norms require one;
- text in headers, footers, charts, or decorative sidebars;
- rating bars and star scores;
- icons in place of contact labels;
- multi-column layouts that scramble extracted text.

Choose section order based on the role. An experienced professional will usually lead with a summary, skills, and experience. A recent graduate may put education before experience. A designer should make the portfolio link easy to find. An executive may use `Executive Summary` and `Leadership Experience` instead of technical headings.
